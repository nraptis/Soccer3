A CONCRETE LEAKAGE ROUTE
A fresh diagnostic stream (seed 0xC346F40A77B09E21, 8192 samples) traced the
strongest predictor's main positional path backward through byte/bit moves.
The separately constructed operation trace matched the production API.

In L1A's third Frog (FrogTwoCipherA), the relevant bytes are 128432 and 128440.
Protected rows are 4 and 5. Interpreting the remaining six writable bits as
a six-bit number, their input values are fixed at 28 and 38. Both receive
the same six-bit key modulo 64 because they share the same mask-byte slot.
Observed states in all 8192 diagnostic samples:

secret 0: key 13, 16, or 45; XOR of the two result high bits = 0
secret 1: key  2, 34, or 63; XOR of the two result high bits = 1

For example, adding 13 produces 41 and 51 (high bits 1,1).
Adding 2 produces 30 and 40 (high bits 0,1).
This is a complete sampled explanation of the intermediate perfect parity,
not a proof that these are all states over the full 2^64 input-flag space.
The six-bit representation is for analysis only; no packing was added.
See trace4-probe/l1a_flag63_addition.csv for counts.

In selection 1's corresponding trace, the possible input/key combinations
occur under BOTH values of the secret. That path stays near chance.

WHY THE ROUTE SURVIVES
Frog repeats eight mask bytes eight times. Two destination bytes eight apart
inside one 64-byte window receive the same added value. At a lowest writable
bit, addition is XOR, so the shared contribution cancels in a two-byte parity.
At higher bits, differences in carry can reveal information about the added
value, as the 28/38 example demonstrates.

L1B places this pair on bit 0. Frog 1 protects bit 0; Frogs 2 and 3 update it
by XOR with the same sampled mask bit in both bytes. The pair parity therefore
survives that entire triplet exactly. A/B packing variants both repeat their
mask eight times, so reversing packing does not remove the repetition.
Fixed XOR material changes a parity only by a fixed inversion. Rotations
move the tracked bits; carries in other stages weaken the correlation but
leave a measurable residual in this arrangement and fixture.

Selection 4 also groups rows 0 and 1 in L1's first Frog. On those rows, the
next two Frogs act as one shared addition modulo 4 for a same-window pair.
That gives an exact low-two-bit modular-difference invariant across that
triplet. It is not by itself the full cause: replacing all L1 arrangements
still leaves a different detectable route.

FIXTURE DEPENDENCE
Fixture 1 has five XOR-style mutations and two add/subtract mutations.
Fixture 2 has one XOR-style mutation and six add/subtract mutations, as well
as different materials and offsets. This is consistent with carry-dependent
leakage and the user's fixture-2 pass, but is not an isolated mutation test.
Selection 4 cannot be declared universally weaker from this one fixture.

HOW STRONG IS 54.8706%?
This is a 4.8706 percentage-point guessing advantage for the best tested bit,
not recovery of 54.87% of the plaintext. The +/-1 correlation is 0.0974121.
It is 798 extra correct guesses over the chance expectation of 8192 in
16384 trials: 12.46875 null-standard-deviation units. The harness's conservative
family-adjusted tail bound is 6.67589e-31. Confirmation uses a disjoint stream.

The learned model uses 8257 chosen-plaintext queries (65 discovery + 4096
training + 4096 validation). Its prediction stage does not query encryption.
This is leakage on a mostly-known fixed template with 64 unknown bits under
one repeated independent-block plan. It does not demonstrate key recovery,
ARX-state recovery, or full arbitrary-archive plaintext recovery.
Repeating the identical deterministic ciphertext does not give new independent
evidence; amplifying a bias requires genuinely new applicable observations.
